# Security and privacy threat model for tuitube (terminal YouTube client using yt-dlp, mpv and maybe a Google account), as of 2026-10-09

Scope note: personal use by one user, defensive focus. "Local test" entries are commands the researcher ran on this Mac (macOS 27.2, build 26B5101f) on 2026-10-09. Source-code findings come from shallow clones made on 2026-10-09: yt-dlp master at commit 51bab8a (2026-09-27; `__version__ = '2026.08.19'`), rustypipe main (last commit 2026-08-03, crate 0.11.4), YouTube.js main (2026-09-23), and the mpv master files fetched raw the same day. Current releases on 2026-10-09: **yt-dlp 2026.08.19** (released 2026-08-19) and **mpv v0.41.0** (released 2025-12-22; v0.40.0 came out 2025-03-25, and only rolling "development builds" have appeared since) ([yt-dlp releases](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/releases); [mpv releases](https://github.com/mpv-player/mpv/releases)).

## Credentials: Google cookies vs. OAuth, infostealers, App-Bound Encryption, DBSC, and whether to be accountless by default

### Takeaway
A YouTube login means Google session cookies (SID/HSID/SSID/APISID/SAPISID/`__Secure-*PSID*`/LOGIN_INFO). These are bearer tokens for the **Google Account**, and YouTube's terms let it suspend the whole Google account. yt-dlp's `--cookies-from-browser` reads **every cookie in the browser profile**, not just YouTube's. Chrome's 2024–2026 defences (App-Bound Encryption, then DBSC, which is on by default for Google accounts on Windows since mid-2026) push cookies out of reach of third-party apps on purpose. OAuth can't stand in either: yt-dlp's OAuth path is dead since late 2024, and the official `youtube.readonly` scope only reaches the Data API, not streams. **Accountless by default is the right call.** Any login should be an explicit opt-in: a throwaway account, pasted cookies held in a 0600 store, never browser extraction.

### Cited Findings
- **What the cookies are.** Google's cookie page says SID and HSID hold "digitally signed and encrypted records of a user's Google Account ID and most recent sign-in time", are used so "only the actual owner of an account can access that account", and last 2 years. YSC lasts for the browser session. PREF (YouTube player settings) expires 8 months after last use. VISITOR_INFO1_LIVE "has been replaced by `__Secure-YNID`", which lasts 6 months. The page doesn't describe SSID, APISID, SAPISID, `__Secure-1PSID/3PSID`, `__Secure-1PSIDTS` or LOGIN_INFO — [Google: How Google uses cookies](https://policies.google.com/technologies/cookies)
- **The blast radius includes the Google account itself.** YouTube's Terms of Service (effective 2023-12-15) say: "YouTube reserves the right to suspend or terminate your Google account or your access to all or part of the Service if…". They also forbid accessing the Service "using any automated means (such as robots, botnets or scrapers)" and anything that would "circumvent, disable, fraudulently engage with, or otherwise interfere with any part of the Service" — [YouTube Terms of Service](https://www.youtube.com/t/terms)
- **Cookie theft aimed at YouTube accounts is old and well documented.** Google TAG (2021-10-20) described "pass-the-cookie" malware campaigns against YouTube creators running since 2019, with hijacked channels resold for $3–$4,000 or used for crypto-scam streams. TAG says the technique resurged because MFA makes other kinds of abuse harder; Google restored about 4K accounts — [Google TAG](https://blog.google/threat-analysis-group/phishing-campaign-targets-youtube-creators-cookie-theft-malware/)
- **The infostealer economy, 2025.** Microsoft's Digital Crimes Unit obtained a court order to seize about 2,300 Lumma Stealer domains (suit filed 2025-05-13, announced 2025-05-21). It counted over 394,000 infected Windows computers between 2025-03-16 and 2025-05-16 — [Microsoft On the Issues](https://blogs.microsoft.com/on-the-issues/2025/05/21/microsoft-leads-global-action-against-favored-cybercrime-tool/)
- **App-Bound Encryption (ABE)** arrived in Chrome 127 (July 2024) to stop malware running as the user from decrypting cookies on Windows. Stealers bypassed it within weeks — [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/infostealer-malware-bypasses-chromes-new-cookie-theft-defenses/). CyberArk's "C4" research showed cookies decrypted as a low-privileged user — [CyberArk](https://www.cyberark.com/resources/home/c4-bomb-blowing-up-chromes-appbound-cookie-encryption). VoidStealer 2.0 (2026-03-13, reported as a non-admin ABE bypass that pulls the master key from Chrome's memory) is single-vendor reporting — [Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/4148612/chrome-encryption-bypass-discovered-new-malware-steals-passwords-and-cookies.html)
- **DBSC timeline.** Chromium announced Device Bound Session Credentials on 2024-04-02, aiming to "substantially reduce the success rate of cookie theft malware" by binding sessions to a key kept in TPM-like hardware — [Chromium/Google blog](https://blog.google/chromium/fighting-cookie-theft-using-device/). Google's 2026-04-09 post said DBSC was publicly available on Windows in Chrome 146 and that macOS would come "in an upcoming Chrome release". It also stated: "there is no reliable way to prevent cookie exfiltration using software alone on any operating system" — [Google Security blog](https://blog.google/security/protecting-cookies-with-device-bound-session-credentials/). The **2026-05-28** Workspace Updates post announced DBSC GA in Chrome for Windows, "enabled by default" for Workspace, Workspace Individual **and personal Google accounts**, with "no administrator control to disable it" and "no end user setting" — [Google Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/05/prevent-account-takeovers-with-DBSC-now-generally-available-in-the-Chrome-browser-for-Windows.html)
- **What DBSC does to copied cookies.** New short-lived session cookies are issued only after Chrome proves it holds a private key that "cannot be exported from the machine", so stolen cookies "expire quickly" — [Google Security blog, 2026-04-09](https://blog.google/security/protecting-cookies-with-device-bound-session-credentials/). BleepingComputer (2026-05-29) notes that stealers (Lumma, Rhadamanthys) had abused Google's OAuth "MultiLogin" endpoint to revive expired Google cookies, which DBSC should block — [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/google-chrome-adds-session-cookie-theft-protection-for-all-users/)
- **DBSC on macOS: unclear.** Aggregators variously cite Chrome 147 or 148 for Mac. Neither Google post confirms a GA date for macOS — [Cybersecurity News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/chromes-device-bound-session-credentials/); [PiunikaWeb, 2026-08-12](https://piunikaweb.com/2026/08/12/chrome-device-bound-session-credentials-account-takeover-protection/)
- **YouTube cookies rotate.** The yt-dlp wiki says "YouTube rotates account cookies frequently on open YouTube browser tabs as a security measure". It recommends exporting from a private window that is "the **only** private/incognito browsing tab open" and is then closed for good, and says *not* to use `--cookies-from-browser` for this — [yt-dlp wiki: Extractors](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/wiki/Extractors). Users report exported cookies lasting "1 - 2 hours" or under a week — [yt-dlp #12009](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/issues/12009); [yt-dlp #13964](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/issues/13964). A downstream project says `__Secure-1PSIDTS/3PSIDTS` rotate per request and old values are soon rejected (weak, single source) — [fuuka-cata-link PR #30](https://github.com/dankhael/fuuka-cata-link/pull/30)
- **`--cookies-from-browser` reads the whole jar.** yt-dlp's Chromium path runs `SELECT host_key, name, value, encrypted_value, path, expires_utc, … FROM cookies` with **no WHERE clause**. Firefox's equivalent reads all of `moz_cookies`. On macOS it gets the decryption key by running `security find-generic-password … -s "<Browser> Safe Storage"`, which triggers the Keychain prompt. No App-Bound (`v20`) handling turned up in the file — [yt-dlp cookies.py](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/yt_dlp/cookies.py)
- **yt-dlp writes cookies back to disk.** `--cookies FILE` is the "Netscape formatted file to read cookies from **and dump cookie jar in**" — [yt-dlp README](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/README.md). `load_cookies()` merges the browser jar and the file jar, and `save_cookies()` calls `cookiejar.save()` whenever a cookie file is set. So `--cookies-from-browser X --cookies f.txt` writes the **entire browser jar** to `f.txt` — [cookies.py](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/yt_dlp/cookies.py); [YoutubeDL.py](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/yt_dlp/YoutubeDL.py)
- **OAuth in yt-dlp is dead.** OAuth via the YouTube TV device flow was added on 2024-10-22 (#11001) and later removed as "broken" (#11558), around Nov–Dec 2024 — [commit mirror](https://gitea.smoothnet.org/nhyatt/yt-dlp/commit/b8635c1d4779da195e71aa281f73aaad702c935e); [commit mirror](https://bytes.keithhacks.cyou/etc/yt-dlp/commit/52c0ffe40ad6e8404d93296f575007b05b04c686). The wiki now says "logging in with OAuth no longer works with yt-dlp" — [yt-dlp wiki: Extractors](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/wiki/Extractors)
- **rustypipe's OAuth.** It still implements a device-code flow with YouTube's TV client ID and scopes `http://gdata.youtube.com https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube`, which is full "Manage your YouTube account" access, not read-only. By default it caches tokens and cookies in `rustypipe_cache.json` **in the current working directory**, set to mode 0600 on Unix — [rustypipe src/client/mod.rs](https://codeberg.org/ThetaDev/rustypipe/src/branch/main/src/client/mod.rs); [src/cache.rs](https://codeberg.org/ThetaDev/rustypipe/src/branch/main/src/cache.rs). Its docs warn "never share the contents of the cache if you are using authentication" — [docs.rs rustypipe](https://docs.rs/rustypipe)
- **The official OAuth route.** The Data API scopes are `youtube` ("Manage your YouTube account"), `youtube.readonly` ("View your YouTube account"), `youtube.force-ssl`, `youtube.upload` and others — [Google OAuth scopes](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/scopes). Apps whose consent screen is still in "Testing" get refresh tokens that "expire after 7 days" — [Google developer setup guide](https://developers.google.com/health/setup). Apps requesting sensitive scopes generally need verification — [Google: sensitive scope verification](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/sensitive-scope-verification). Default quota is 10,000 units/day. The current page lists `search.list` at **1 unit, capped at 100 calls/day**, and subscriptions/playlistItems/videos.list at 1 unit — [YouTube Data API quota costs](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/determine_quota_cost)

### Inferences
- **Threat ranking for tuitube's own credential store.** (1) A same-user infostealer or a poisoned dependency reading the store. (2) Accidental leaks: logs, crash dumps, `ps` argv, a cookie file in a synced folder or CWD. (3) Account action by Google for unofficial use. The first two are why the store must never hold anything wider than YouTube needs.
- **Rule: tuitube never calls `--cookies-from-browser`.** It loads the whole browser jar (banking, email, Drive) into a Python process, can trigger a Keychain prompt the user may approve by reflex, and, combined with `--cookies`, writes all of it to disk. The same "never touch other apps' secrets" principle tuigram applies.
- **If a login is ever offered, model it on tuimeta's cookie paste.** The user pastes a Netscape cookies.txt exported from a dedicated private window of a **throwaway** Google account (the yt-dlp wiki's own advice). tuitube filters it to `.youtube.com` cookies, checks the required names are present, masks input, and stores it in the 0700 data dir as 0600 (or wrapped with the OS keystore, as the tuigram report recommends). It reaches yt-dlp as a `--cookies` path inside that 0700 dir, never via argv values or env. Because yt-dlp rewrites the file with rotated values, that file is the live copy. The login screen should say: this is a separate account; YouTube may suspend the Google account; cookies grant full account access.
- **DBSC narrows the cookie route.** Cookies exported from a normal, DBSC-bound Chrome session will probably stop working within minutes to hours away from that browser, which matches the short lifetimes users report. Even a working cookie login is fragile and maintenance-heavy, which argues further for accountless by default. (Inference: no source tests DBSC against yt-dlp, and whether incognito sessions are DBSC-bound is unknown.)
- **OAuth `youtube.readonly` with the user's own Google Cloud project is the least-privilege way to import subscriptions and playlists once.** It can't fetch streams and doesn't touch Gmail or Drive, but Testing-mode tokens expire weekly. Don't copy rustypipe's TV-client OAuth: it impersonates YouTube's app and grants the full `youtube` scope.
- **Accountless default.** Keep subscriptions, playlists, watch-later and history locally (the FreeTube/NewPipe model, see the privacy section) and offer a one-time import (Google Takeout CSV or Data API `youtube.readonly`), not a live session.

### Gaps
- No primary source confirms whether a `.youtube.com` copy of SID/`__Secure-1PSID` can be replayed against `mail.google.com`/Drive. Treat YouTube cookies as full-account credentials until shown otherwise.
- The macOS DBSC GA date and Chrome version were not confirmed from a Google source. Whether DBSC covers incognito sessions, Firefox or Safari sessions is undocumented.
- Google's cookie page does not document SAPISID, `__Secure-1PSID/3PSID`, `__Secure-1PSIDTS` or LOGIN_INFO.
- Whether the Data API exposes watch history: believed removed years ago, not verified here.

## Hostile content from YouTube: titles, descriptions, comments, captions, links, thumbnails

### Takeaway
Everything YouTube returns is attacker-controlled text: titles, descriptions, comments, channel names, chapter titles, playlist names, captions. Terminal escape injection keeps producing CVEs (2024 iTerm2/Ghostty title-report RCEs; 2025–2026 Rust/CLI tools printing untrusted strings), so tuimeta's `text::clean` rules carry over unchanged. Two YouTube-specific additions. **Links:** description and comment links arrive wrapped in `youtube.com/redirect?…&q=` with truncated display text, so the "disguised link" rule must compare hosts after unwrapping. **Images:** thumbnails should decode with a minimal pure-Rust feature set (JPEG + WebP) within strict limits, never through a C decoder like libwebp or libdav1d.

### Cited Findings
- **Terminal escape injection: the RCE class.** CVE-2024-38396 (iTerm2 3.5.x before 3.5.2, June 2024): an unfiltered title-report escape sequence combined with the default tmux integration allowed injecting arbitrary code into the terminal — [oss-security 2024-06-17](https://openwall.com/lists/oss-security/2024/06/17/1); [Tenable](https://www.tenable.com/cve/CVE-2024-38396). CVE-2024-56803 (Ghostty 1.0.0, Dec 2024): a title set by escape sequence and reported back onto the command line, executed when the user pressed Enter; fixed in 1.0.1 — [NVD](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2024-56803)
- **The same class in 2025–2026 tools.** CVE-2025-58160 (Rust `tracing-subscriber` < 0.3.20): ANSI injection via logged user input; the advisory notes terminal-emulator bugs make this exploitable — [NVD](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2025-58160). CVE-2026-45803 (GitHub CLI `gh run view --log` streaming unsanitized control sequences; fixed 2.92.0) — [OSV](https://osv.dev/vulnerability/CVE-2026-45803). CVE-2026-47090: unencoded values in OSC 8 hyperlinks enabled prompt forging, OSC 52 clipboard writes and outbound requests — [OSV](https://osv.dev/vulnerability/CVE-2026-47090). Deno itself shipped permission-prompt spoofing via improper ANSI stripping (CVE-2023-28446, CVE-2024-27936) — [OSV query, crates.io `deno`](https://osv.dev/list?ecosystem=crates.io&q=deno)
- **Comment and description links are wrapped and truncated.** YouTube wraps them as `https://www.youtube.com/redirect?event=video_description&redir_token=…&q=<target>`. rustypipe has a dedicated function to "Convert YouTube redirect URLs … into regular URLs", and its fixtures show display text such as `https://www.ticketmaster.com/event/0A...` (truncated with "...") pointing at a redirect URL — [rustypipe src/util/mod.rs](https://codeberg.org/ThetaDev/rustypipe/src/branch/main/src/util/mod.rs); [rustypipe src/model/richtext.rs](https://codeberg.org/ThetaDev/rustypipe/src/branch/main/src/model/richtext.rs)
- **ID formats.** Video IDs match `[0-9A-Za-z_-]{11}`, so an ID can start with `-`. Playlist IDs use known prefixes (`PL|LL|EC|UU|FL|RD|UL|TL|PU|OLAK5uy_`) plus `[0-9A-Za-z-_]{10,}` — [yt-dlp youtube/_video.py](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/yt_dlp/extractor/youtube/_video.py); [_base.py](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/yt_dlp/extractor/youtube/_base.py)
- **Thumbnail decoder risk: the C precedent.** CVE-2023-4863, a heap overflow in libwebp (published 2023-09-12), was exploited in the wild: CISA KEV added it on 2023-09-13 — [OSV CVE-2023-4863](https://osv.dev/vulnerability/CVE-2023-4863); [CISA KEV catalog](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)
- **The `image` crate's attack surface.** The `image` crate (0.25.9 on main) enables by default `avif, bmp, exr, ff, gif, hdr, ico, jpeg, png, pnm, qoi, tga, tiff, webp`. JPEG decodes via `zune-jpeg` and WebP via the pure-Rust `image-webp`. The default `avif` feature is the `ravif` *encoder*; AVIF *decoding* needs `avif-native`, which links the C `libdav1d` — [image Cargo.toml](https://github.com/image-rs/image/blob/main/Cargo.toml)
- **`image::Limits`.** Only `max_image_width`/`max_image_height` are strict. `max_alloc` (default 512 MiB) "is non-strict by default and some decoders may ignore it" — [docs.rs image::Limits](https://docs.rs/image/latest/image/struct.Limits.html)
- **Rust advisories.** An OSV query on 2026-10-09 found no advisories published since 2023 for `image`, `image-webp`, `zune-jpeg`, `png`, `gif`, `ratatui`, `crossterm` or `open` — [OSV](https://osv.dev/list?ecosystem=crates.io). Pure-Rust image decoders are not immune: RUSTSEC-2026-0151 (2026-05-29) is an integer overflow leading to out-of-bounds writes in `jxl-grid` (JPEG XL) — [OSV RUSTSEC-2026-0151](https://osv.dev/vulnerability/RUSTSEC-2026-0151)

### Inferences
- **Carry tuimeta's rules over unchanged.** Every remote string (title, channel name, handle, description, comment and author, chapter title, playlist name, caption line, error text from yt-dlp/mpv stderr, search suggestions) goes through `text::clean` before display, copy or notification. That means stripping C0/C1 controls except `\n`/`\t`, ESC-initiated sequences (CSI/OSC/DCS/APC, including OSC 8 and OSC 52), bidi overrides/isolates/marks, and zero-width/invisible characters. Sizes are capped: titles about 300 chars on one line, descriptions and comments truncated with an "expand" action, caption lines capped, and total bytes per response capped before parsing.
- **Captions.** If tuitube renders captions itself (rather than mpv/libass), they are attacker-authored text arriving at playback rate. Same cleaning, plus caps on cue count and line length. If mpv renders them, mpv/libass handle them, not the terminal.
- **Links.** Unwrap `youtube.com/redirect?…q=` locally (opening the wrapper tells YouTube about the click and shows an interstitial), then apply tuimeta's `web_url` (http/https only) and `Confirm` with the host on its own line. Because YouTube truncates display text with "...", a plain "text ≠ URL" disguise test would fire on every long link. Instead, treat a link as disguised when the display text *looks like a URL or domain* and its host differs from the target's host. Timestamps (`&t=`) and `/watch?v=`, `/@handle`, `/channel/UC…` and `/playlist?list=` links should become in-app navigation, never handed to the OS.
- **Homoglyph and impersonation display.** Channel names can imitate verified channels. Show the `@handle` beside the display name, and don't render name-supplied emoji or "✓" in a way that looks like YouTube's verified badge. Draw verification only from a structured field, in a colour a name can't produce (as tuimeta does with the 🔒).
- **Thumbnails.** Build `image` with `default-features = false, features = ["jpeg", "webp"]` (plus `png` if channel avatars need it), never `avif-native`. Set strict width/height limits (YouTube thumbnails are at most a few thousand pixels wide; channel banners are larger, so skip them). Check `Content-Length` before downloading, cap bytes, decode on a blocking thread inside `catch_unwind` (tuimeta's `panic_is_contained`), and fetch only from `i.ytimg.com`/`yt3.ggpht.com`-style hosts. Thumbnails should be optional and off in a "paranoid" profile.
- **Video IDs.** Validate with `^[0-9A-Za-z_-]{11}$` and playlist and channel IDs with their own regexes before they reach a subprocess. Build canonical URLs (`https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=<id>`) in Rust. Never pass a URL taken from remote data to yt-dlp or mpv: the yt-dlp proxy-smuggling CVE in the next sections came in exactly that way.

### Gaps
- No YouTube-specific escape-injection incident was found. The risk comes from the general bug class, not a recorded attack on YouTube clients.
- Which hosts serve every thumbnail and avatar variant (`i.ytimg.com`, `yt3.ggpht.com`, `yt3.googleusercontent.com`) was not verified from a source. Confirm empirically with a logging proxy.

## mpv attack surface: media parsers, argument injection, config and scripts, URL schemes, JSON IPC

### Takeaway
mpv sits on top of FFmpeg, which fixes memory-safety CVEs in every release. Some hit stream formats YouTube actually uses: a DASH manifest out-of-bounds write (CVE-2025-59728) and an AV1 decoder out-of-bounds read (CVE-2026-30997). mpv's main risks for tuitube come from integration choices, not mpv bugs. mpv auto-loads `~/.config/mpv` (config, Lua/JS scripts, `ytdl-raw-options`). It accepts legacy single-dash options, so an ID starting with `-` can be read as an option. Its JSON IPC is "explicitly insecure", with a `run` command that executes arbitrary programs. Launch mpv with `--no-config`, `--ytdl=no`, a `--` separator and an IPC socket inside a 0700 directory (or an inherited fd), and hand it only resolved googlevideo URLs.

### Cited Findings
- **The JSON IPC is insecure by design.** It is "not intended to be a secure network protocol. It is explicitly insecure: there is no authentication, no encryption", and "the `run` command is exposed, which can run arbitrary system commands". Its intended use is "controlling the player locally" — [mpv DOCS/man/ipc.rst](https://github.com/mpv-player/mpv/blob/master/DOCS/man/ipc.rst)
- **Unix socket creation.** mpv calls `fchmod(ipc_fd, 0600)` *before* `bind()` and doesn't check the result. There's no `umask()` call, it `unlink()`s any existing file at the path unconditionally, and it does no peer-credential check (`accept(ipc_fd, NULL, NULL)`). A path beginning with `@` becomes a Linux abstract-namespace socket, which has no filesystem permissions at all. `fd://N` is supported only for `--input-ipc-client` (an already-connected socket) — [mpv input/ipc-unix.c](https://github.com/mpv-player/mpv/blob/master/input/ipc-unix.c)
- **Windows named pipe.** `\\.\pipe\` is prefixed automatically. The pipe uses `PIPE_REJECT_REMOTE_CLIENTS` and a security descriptor granting `GENERIC_READ|GENERIC_WRITE` only to the current user's SID, with a mandatory label denying lower integrity levels. The code comment says it "only grants access to processes running under the current user at the current integrity level or higher" — [mpv input/ipc-win.c](https://github.com/mpv-player/mpv/blob/master/input/ipc-win.c)
- **Legacy option syntax.** "The alternative legacy syntax `-option value` and `-option=value` will also work", so `mpv -fs no` plays a file named `no` — [mpv.rst](https://github.com/mpv-player/mpv/blob/master/DOCS/man/mpv.rst). The command-line parser treats a bare `--` as the end of options (`no_more_opts = true`) — [mpv options/parse_commandline.c](https://github.com/mpv-player/mpv/blob/master/options/parse_commandline.c)
- **Config and scripts load automatically.** `~/.config/mpv/mpv.conf`, `input.conf`, `fonts.conf` and `scripts/` are read (and `~/.mpv/` too if present). "All files in [`scripts/`] are loaded as if they were passed to the `--script` option… The `--load-scripts=no` option disables loading these files." `$MPV_HOME`/`$XDG_CONFIG_HOME` move the directory. Watch-later state is written under `~/.local/state/mpv/watch_later/`. `--no-config` makes all config-dir paths empty — [mpv.rst FILES section](https://github.com/mpv-player/mpv/blob/master/DOCS/man/mpv.rst); [mpv manual](https://mpv.io/manual/master/)
- **What ytdl_hook does.** It builds `yt-dlp --no-warnings -J --flat-playlist --sub-format ass/srt/best [--format F]`, appends **every `ytdl-raw-options` entry verbatim as `--<param> <arg>`**, then `--sub-langs all --write-srt [--no-playlist] -- <url>`. It does **not** pass `--ignore-config`. The hook runs only for `http(s)://` URLs (or `ytdl://`). Returned stream URLs must use a "safe" protocol (`http, https, ftp, ftps, rtmp*, data`). Cookies from yt-dlp's JSON are handed to mpv as an in-memory `memory://` cookies file — [mpv player/lua/ytdl_hook.lua](https://github.com/mpv-player/mpv/blob/master/player/lua/ytdl_hook.lua). The manual says `ytdl_path` is searched "in PATH and in mpv's config directory" — [mpv manual](https://mpv.io/manual/master/)
- **Playlist safety.** `--load-unsafe-playlists` defaults to no. "Local files and HTTP links… are always considered safe", while special protocols such as `avdevice://` are "inherently unsafe" — [mpv manual](https://mpv.io/manual/master/)
- **mpv's own CVE history is short.** CVE-2018-6360: the youtube-dl hook accepted URLs without a protocol whitelist, allowing code execution via a crafted page (mpv ≤ 0.28.0) — [NVD](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2018-6360). CVE-2021-30145 is a format-string bug via a crafted m3u playlist in mpv ≤ 0.33.0 — [Rapid7](https://www.rapid7.com/db/vulnerabilities/ubuntu-cve-2021-30145/). OSV-2025-591 (2025-08-02) is an OSS-Fuzz unknown-read crash in `mov_read_header` via `demux_open_lavf`, with no CVE — [OSV](https://osv.dev/vulnerability/OSV-2025-591). No mpv-specific CVE for 2025–2026 was found.
- **FFmpeg CVEs in YouTube's formats.** FFmpeg's security page lists CVEs fixed per release: 7.1.2, 8.0 through 8.0.3, 8.1/8.1.2 and 9.0, with 2025 and 2026 IDs throughout — [ffmpeg.org/security.html](https://ffmpeg.org/security.html). Relevant to YouTube media: **CVE-2025-59728**, an out-of-bounds NUL write when handling MPEG-DASH manifests (published 2025-10-06), and **CVE-2026-30997**, an out-of-bounds read in `libavcodec/av1dec.c` `read_global_param()` in FFmpeg 8.0.1 (published 2026-04-13). Many others sit in codecs YouTube never serves (SANM, OpenEXR, DHAV, RV60, JPEG2000: CVE-2025-59729–59734, CVE-2025-69693, CVE-2025-9951) — [OSV CVE-2025-59728](https://osv.dev/vulnerability/CVE-2025-59728); [OSV CVE-2026-30997](https://osv.dev/vulnerability/CVE-2026-30997); [OSV CVE-2025-9951](https://osv.dev/vulnerability/CVE-2025-9951). No FFmpeg or mpv entry appears in CISA's KEV catalog (checked 2026-10-09) — [CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)

### Inferences
- **mpv invocation rule (one function, `player::spawn`).** `mpv --no-config --load-scripts=no --ytdl=no --idle=yes --input-ipc-server=<private path> -- <resolved stream URLs>`, with `Command::new` and an argv vector, never a shell. `--no-config` alone already prevents `mpv.conf` from adding `ytdl-raw-options=exec=…`, `script=…` or a planted `yt-dlp` in the config dir. `--load-scripts=no` is belt-and-braces. Keep the user's mpv config as an explicit opt-in toggle ("use my mpv config, which can run scripts"), off by default. (Whether to also pass `--no-input-default-bindings` is a UX choice, not a security one.)
- **`--ytdl=no` plus tuitube resolving streams itself.** tuitube runs yt-dlp once with its own hardened arguments (see the yt-dlp section) and passes mpv the googlevideo stream URLs. Otherwise mpv's ytdl_hook would launch a second yt-dlp that reads the user's yt-dlp config (no `--ignore-config`), picks up raw options and gets cookies via `memory://`. If ytdl_hook is used anyway, pass `--script-opts=ytdl_hook-ytdl_path=<absolute pinned path>` and `--ytdl-raw-options=ignore-config=,no-plugin-dirs=`.
- **Argument injection.** Always put `--` before positional URLs or files, for mpv and yt-dlp alike, and only ever pass `https://` URLs tuitube built itself or that yt-dlp returned and that pass a host allowlist (`*.googlevideo.com`, `manifest.googlevideo.com`). Reject anything starting with `-` or containing control characters, as defence in depth beyond the ID regex.
- **IPC placement.** Linux: `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/tuitube/` (0700) or a fresh 0700 dir under the data dir, socket name random per launch, never an `@abstract` name, and set `umask(0o077)` in the child before exec. macOS: `fchmod` before `bind` doesn't take effect on BSD-derived systems (an inference about platform behaviour), so the enclosing 0700 directory is the real protection; use a short path, since `sun_path` is about 104 bytes on macOS. Cleaner still on Unix: create a `socketpair()` and pass one end as `--input-ipc-client=fd://N`, so there is no filesystem object at all (supported per ipc.rst/ipc-unix.c). Windows: mpv's pipe DACL is already current-user-only. Use a random pipe name, and for tuitube's own client code note tokio's earlier `reject_remote_clients` bug (CVE-2023-22466) — [OSV](https://osv.dev/vulnerability/RUSTSEC-2023-0001).
- **Treat mpv as untrusted after it opens media.** A malicious stream exploiting FFmpeg runs as the user unless mpv is sandboxed (see the sandboxing section). Keep mpv updated through the OS package manager, and record the minimum mpv/FFmpeg versions in `--version` checks. A narrow FFmpeg format and protocol whitelist via `--demuxer-lavf-o=protocol_whitelist=…`/`format_whitelist=…` is a possible hardening step (unverified; see Gaps).
- **Never send `run`, `subprocess`, `load-script` or `loadfile` with remote strings over IPC.** tuitube's IPC client should expose a typed allowlist of commands (pause, seek, set volume, observe property) and never forward user-typed or remote text as an mpv command.

### Gaps
- Not verified: whether mpv's own Matroska/WebM demuxer or libavformat handles each YouTube container, and whether `format_whitelist`/`protocol_whitelist` via `--demuxer-lavf-o` breaks DASH/HLS playback in mpv.
- The macOS `fchmod`-before-`bind` behaviour was inferred, not tested.
- mpv publishes no security advisories, so 2025–2026 fixes may be unlabeled in commits.

## yt-dlp attack surface: CVEs, config and plugins, JS challenge runtime, updates and distribution

### Takeaway
yt-dlp has had 13 security advisories (2023-07 to 2026-07). Almost all live in features tuitube shouldn't use: `--exec`, `--netrc-cmd`, external downloaders (aria2c, curl), `--write-link`, filename templates, and the generic extractor's URL smuggling. The structural risks are **silent configuration** (config files including one in the CWD; plugins imported from many directories without checks) and the new **JavaScript runtime**: YouTube's player JS is run in Deno with no permissions by default. Pin a version, verify hashes, and invoke it with `--ignore-config --no-plugin-dirs`, `YTDLP_NO_PLUGINS=1`, `--no-remote-components`, a private CWD and an explicit Deno path.

### Cited Findings
- **Full advisory list (OSV, PyPI `yt-dlp`, queried 2026-10-09)** — [OSV API](https://osv.dev/list?ecosystem=PyPI&q=yt-dlp); [yt-dlp Security tab](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/security):
  - GHSA-v8mc-9377-rwjj / **CVE-2023-35934** (2023-07-06, fixed 2023.07.06): cookie leak. Cookies were passed to downloaders as a `Cookie` header and leaked on cross-host redirects or when fragment hosts differed from the manifest host. Affected all youtube-dl/yt-dlp releases since 2015.01.25.
  - GHSA-42h4-v29r-42qg / **CVE-2023-40581** (2023-09-25, fixed 2023.09.24): Windows `--exec` command injection with `%q`.
  - GHSA-3ch3-jhc6-5r8x / **CVE-2023-46121** (2023-11-15, fixed 2023.11.14): the generic extractor accepted "url smuggling" data in the URL fragment, letting an attacker set an arbitrary proxy for a request and MITM it, which "could lead to cookie exfiltration".
  - GHSA-hjq6-52gw-2g7p / **CVE-2024-22423** (2024-04-10, fixed 2024.04.09): bypass of CVE-2023-40581.
  - GHSA-79w7-vh3h-8g4j / **CVE-2024-38519** (2024-07-02, fixed 2024.07.01): file system modification and RCE through improper file-extension sanitization.
  - GHSA-3v33-3wmw-3785 (2024-07-08, fixed 2024.07.07): Douyu extractors depended on potentially malicious third-party code.
  - GHSA-45hg-7f49-5h56 (2025-07-21): Windows `--exec` placeholder injection, a bypass of CVE-2024-22423 — [GitHub advisories](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/security) (not in OSV's PyPI list).
  - GHSA-g3gw-q23r-pgqm / **CVE-2026-26331** (2026-02-23, fixed 2026.02.21): command injection via `--netrc-cmd`.
  - GHSA-69qj-pvh9-c5wg (2026-06-16, fixed 2026.06.09): `--exec` with `%(title)s`-style templates allows "arbitrary command injection when handling untrusted metadata".
  - GHSA-vx4q-3cr2-7cg2 / **CVE-2026-50574** (fixed 2026.06.09): HLS/DASH via aria2c allows arbitrary file write, giving code execution on Windows immediately or on the next yt-dlp run elsewhere.
  - GHSA-f7j3-774f-rfhj / **CVE-2026-50019** (fixed 2026.06.09): cookie leak with the curl downloader, present since 2023.09.24.
  - GHSA-c6mh-fpjc-4pr3 / **CVE-2026-50023** (fixed 2026.06.09): filename sanitization bypass of CVE-2024-38519.
  - GHSA-6v4j-43gg-vj32 / **CVE-2026-55404** (2026-07-24, fixed 2026.07.04): `--write-link`/`--write-desktop-link` produce `.url`/`.desktop` files with injected commands.
- **No SECURITY.md.** GitHub showed "No security policy detected" for yt-dlp on 2026-10-09 (reports go through GitHub's private advisory form) — [yt-dlp Security](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/security)
- **Config files load silently.** Main (`--config-locations`); **portable** (`yt-dlp.conf` next to the binary); **home** (`yt-dlp.conf` in the `-P` home path, "If `-P` is not given, **the current directory is searched**"); user (`$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/yt-dlp/config`, `~/yt-dlp.conf`, `~/.yt-dlp/config`, `%APPDATA%\yt-dlp\config`, …); system (`/etc/yt-dlp.conf`, …). `--ignore-config` (alias `--no-config`) means "Don't load any more configuration files except those given to `--config-locations`" — [yt-dlp README](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/README.md)
- **Plugins load from many directories, unchecked.** "**all** plugins are imported even if not invoked, and… **there are no checks** performed on plugin code". "Extractor plugins take priority over built-in extractors" and run automatically for matching URLs. They load from `${XDG_CONFIG_HOME}/yt-dlp/plugins/`, `~/.yt-dlp/plugins/`, `~/yt-dlp-plugins/`, `/etc/yt-dlp/plugins/`, a `yt-dlp-plugins` dir next to the executable, and pip-installed packages. "Set the environment variable `YTDLP_NO_PLUGINS`… to disable loading plugins entirely". `--no-plugin-dirs` clears search dirs — [yt-dlp README](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/README.md)
- **The JS challenge runtime (EJS).** YouTube's player JS challenges are solved by the `yt-dlp-ejs` scripts run in an external runtime. "Only 'deno' is enabled by default"; priority order is deno, node, quickjs, bun. `--remote-components` (`ejs:npm`, `ejs:github`) is off by default ("By default, no remote components are allowed") and not needed with official binaries or a matching `yt-dlp-ejs` package — [yt-dlp README](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/README.md). The wiki says Deno code runs with restricted permissions ("no file system or network access"), Node "with some permissions restricted", Bun with **no restrictions**, and QuickJS via a temp file per run ("could enable a TOCTOU vulnerability"). Minimum versions: Deno 2.3.0, Node 22.0.0. The ejs scripts are bundled in official PyInstaller/zipimport binaries or come from the `yt-dlp-ejs` PyPI package — [yt-dlp wiki: EJS](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/wiki/EJS)
- **Exact Deno and Node invocations.** Deno runs as `deno run --ext=js --no-code-cache --no-prompt --no-remote --no-lock --node-modules-dir=none --no-config [--no-npm --cached-only] -` with **no `--allow-*` flags** (Deno's default-deny), with the script on stdin. The environment is a copy of `os.environ` plus proxy variables. Node gets `--permission` (≥ 23.5.0) or `--experimental-permission`. Bun runs as `bun --bun run … -` — [deno.py](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/yt_dlp/extractor/youtube/jsc/_builtin/deno.py); [node.py](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/yt_dlp/extractor/youtube/jsc/_builtin/node.py); [bun.py](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/yt_dlp/extractor/youtube/jsc/_builtin/bun.py)
- **Deno's permission layer has a steady bypass record.** Most bugs concern *partial* grants: `--allow-read` plus `--deny-read` (CVE-2025-48888), `--deny-write`/`--deny-read` bypasses (CVE-2025-61785/61786, Oct 2025), `node:sqlite` read/write bypass (CVE-2025-48935), `fetch()`/WebSocket "sandbox bypass via missing DNS resolution check" (CVE-2026-49859/49860, 2026-06-16), APFS Unicode-normalization permission bypass on macOS (CVE-2026-49401, 2026-06-16), and env-permission bypass via `process.loadEnvFile()` (CVE-2026-49983) — [OSV, crates.io `deno`](https://osv.dev/list?ecosystem=crates.io&q=deno)
- **Self-update integrity.** The `-U` updater downloads `SHA2-256SUMS` from the same GitHub release and compares the binary's SHA-256 (`hashlib.sha256(newcontent).hexdigest() != update_info.checksum`). No GPG verification appears in `update.py` — [yt-dlp update.py](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/yt_dlp/update.py). Releases also publish `SHA2-256SUMS.sig`/`SHA2-512SUMS.sig` signed with a published GPG key, which users can verify by hand. For `--update-to <owner/repo>`, "there is no verification done for binaries from different repositories". Channels are `stable` (often "stale"), `nightly` ("recommended channel for regular users") and `master` — [yt-dlp README](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/README.md)
- **yt-dlp's release pipeline.** Release workflows pin actions by full SHA (e.g. `actions/checkout@3d3c42e…  # v7.0.1`) and publish to PyPI with Trusted Publishing (`id-token: write`, `pypa/gh-action-pypi-publish@dc37677…`) — [release.yml](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/.github/workflows/release.yml)
- **PO tokens.** These are bound to "the user session (Visitor ID or account Session ID) or to the video ID". The guide recommends third-party providers (bgutil, which runs BotGuard attestation; getpot-wpc, which drives a browser), "not affiliated with yt-dlp". Missing tokens may "result in your account or IP address being blocked" — [yt-dlp wiki: PO Token Guide](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/wiki/PO-Token-Guide) (last edited 2026-07-12)
- **A YouTube-client supply-chain precedent.** SmartTube (Android TV) had its signing key and likely its dev machine compromised in Nov–Dec 2025. Builds carried `libalphasdk.so`, which collected device data, and its **built-in updater delivered infected APKs**. A new key and package name followed in 30.56 — [CyberInsider](https://cyberinsider.com/popular-youtube-app-for-android-tv-smarttube-compromised-with-malware/); [AhnLab ASEC](https://asec.ahnlab.com/en/91414/)

### Inferences
- **yt-dlp invocation rule (one function, `extractor::run_ytdlp`).**
  - **Binary:** an absolute path to a pinned yt-dlp, not whatever `yt-dlp` is on `PATH`.
  - **Working directory and env:** CWD set to a fresh 0700 dir, which defeats `./yt-dlp.conf`. Env cleared down to `PATH`, `HOME`, `LANG` and `YTDLP_NO_PLUGINS=1`, with `HOME`/`XDG_CONFIG_HOME` optionally pointed at a private dir so user config and plugins are invisible even if a flag is missed.
  - **Config and plugin flags:** `--ignore-config --no-plugin-dirs --no-remote-components`.
  - **JS runtime:** `--no-js-runtimes --js-runtimes deno:<absolute path>`.
  - **Output and safety flags:** `--no-mark-watched` (the default, stated explicitly), `-J`/`--print` for JSON only, `--no-cache-dir` or a private cache dir, then `--` and the canonical URL.
  - **Never:** `--exec`, `--netrc-cmd`, `--external-downloader`, `--write-link`, output templates built from metadata, `--cookies-from-browser`, or `--update`.
- **Prefer resolve-only.** yt-dlp hands back format URLs and mpv plays them, so yt-dlp's downloaders, which carry most of the CVEs, never run. Offline downloads, if added later, should use yt-dlp's native downloader, a fixed `-o` template using only `%(id)s.%(ext)s` inside a 0700 dir, and tuitube's own display-name mapping.
- **Deno.** Keep it as the only runtime and refuse Bun (no sandbox) and QuickJS (temp-file TOCTOU). Since yt-dlp grants Deno *no* permissions, the partial-grant bypasses above mostly don't apply; a V8 memory-corruption bug would still escape the policy layer entirely. That's an argument for OS-level sandboxing of the yt-dlp process tree. Because yt-dlp copies its whole environment into Deno, tuitube must never put secrets in yt-dlp's env.
- **Updates.** tuitube should never run `yt-dlp -U` (the SmartTube lesson: a compromised updater ships to everyone). Pin a release in tuitube's config with its SHA-256 (or verify `SHA2-256SUMS.sig` against the pinned GPG key on download) and update deliberately. Since stable is "often stale" against YouTube changes, offer a "check for newer yt-dlp" command that shows the version, release date and verification result before replacing anything. Homebrew/pip users: record the expected version and warn on mismatch.
- **Optional PO-token providers are a second extractor ecosystem**: Node and BotGuard code from third parties. Not by default; if added, they go through the same sandbox and pinning.

### Gaps
- Whether yt-dlp publishes GitHub artifact attestations for release binaries wasn't confirmed: the workflows reference `gh attestation verify` in tests, but release attestation wasn't checked.
- The exact Deno version shipped or required by yt-dlp 2026.08.19 wasn't checked beyond the wiki's 2.3.0 minimum.

## Rust supply chain and CI: cargo-deny/audit/vet, TLS choice, GitHub Actions hardening, provenance

### Takeaway
crates.io saw malicious crates aimed at credentials through 2025–2026, including the **2026-08-20 arrayref compromise**: a long-established crate republished with a build-script dropper. So the tuigram controls (`--locked`, reviewed lockfile diffs, cargo-deny/RustSec in CI, a minimal-feature dependency tree) are needed from day one. Add cargo-vet for new transitive crates, SHA-pinned Actions with least-privilege tokens (now enforceable by GitHub policy since 2025-08-15), and `actions/attest` provenance for releases.

### Cited Findings
- **arrayref (2026-08-20).** The Rust Security Response Team found `proc-macro1`'s build script downloading a payload. `arrayref` 0.3.10 had been republished depending on it, with legitimate versions yanked to steer users to it; `internment` and `append-only-vec` were hit the same way. They stayed live for 86–107 minutes, and the maintainer's account was locked — [Linuxiac](https://linuxiac.com/rust-supply-chain-attack-compromises-popular-arrayref-crate/); [StepSecurity](https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/arrayref-rust-crate-supply-chain-attack); [RUSTSEC-2026-0264](https://rustsec.org/advisories/RUSTSEC-2026-0264) (`proc-macro-en`, same build script, 2026-08-20). One vendor report links the infrastructure to DPRK activity (unconfirmed by the Rust team) — [CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-arrayref-rust-supply-chain-dprk-overlap-20/)
- **Other malicious crates.** RUSTSEC-2025-0148 (2025-12-05): `finch-rust`, depending on `sha-rust`, which tried to steal credentials from local files — [OSV](https://osv.dev/vulnerability/RUSTSEC-2025-0148). RUSTSEC-2026-0102 (2026-04-13): `microsoftsystem64` installed an SSH `authorized_keys` backdoor, scanned for `.env`/credential files and exfiltrated them — [OSV](https://osv.dev/vulnerability/RUSTSEC-2026-0102). The September 2025 `faster_log`/`async_println` crates (about 8,500 downloads) stole wallet keys (secondary source) — [Aviatrix threat research](https://aviatrix.ai/threat-research-center/rust-crates-2025-malicious-packages-crypto-wallet-theft)
- **crates.io Trusted Publishing.** It launched in July 2025 (GitHub Actions, via OIDC) — [Rust blog 2025-07-11](https://blog.rust-lang.org/2025/07/11/crates-io-development-update-2025-07). Later updates added GitLab, a "Trusted Publishing Only" mode, and blocked `pull_request_target`/`workflow_run` triggers — [Rust blog 2026-01-21](https://blog.rust-lang.org/2026/01/21/crates-io-development-update)
- **cargo-vet** "matches all of a project's third-party dependencies against a set of audits", imports audits from others, and keeps a shrinking exemptions list — [cargo-vet book](https://mozilla.github.io/cargo-vet/)
- **TLS crate advisories.** rustls has had network-reachable DoS/panic advisories (RUSTSEC-2024-0336 on 2024-04-19; RUSTSEC-2024-0399 on 2024-11-22) and **RUSTSEC-2026-0285 (2026-09-14)**: TLS 1.3 handshake messages accepted across encryption-level boundaries — [OSV](https://osv.dev/vulnerability/RUSTSEC-2026-0285). reqwest 0.13.0 made **rustls the default TLS backend** with the aws-lc crypto provider and `rustls-platform-verifier` for roots (changelog via secondary sources) — [newreleases.io reqwest 0.13.0-rc.1](https://newreleases.io/project/cargo/reqwest/release/0.13.0-rc.1). rustypipe's default feature is `default-tls` (native-tls), with rustls features available — [rustypipe Cargo.toml](https://codeberg.org/ThetaDev/rustypipe/src/branch/main/Cargo.toml)
- **GitHub Actions.** The tj-actions/changed-files compromise (CVE-2025-30066, March 2025) repointed nearly all version tags to a malicious commit. Users pinned to full SHAs weren't affected — [Orca Security](https://orca.security/resources/blog/github-action-tj-actions-changed-files-compromised/). Since **2025-08-15** org and repo admins can enforce full-SHA pinning and block actions through the allowed-actions policy — [GitHub changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-08-15-github-actions-policy-now-supports-blocking-and-sha-pinning-actions/). The check runs at workflow run time, not at PR merge — [zenn.dev hands-on](https://zenn.dev/shunsuke_suzuki/articles/github-actions-enforce-sha-pinning)
- **Artifact attestations.** Build provenance comes from `actions/attest@v4` with `id-token: write`, `contents: read`, `attestations: write`, and is verified with `gh attestation verify <file> -R owner/repo` — [GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/how-tos/secure-your-work/use-artifact-attestations/use-artifact-attestations)

### Inferences
- **Baseline CI:**
  - `cargo build --locked`, with the release profile built from a clean checkout.
  - `cargo deny check advisories bans licenses sources`: deny git and unknown registries, deny yanked crates, ban duplicate TLS stacks, and ban `openssl-sys` if going pure rustls.
  - A `cargo audit` or cargo-deny RustSec check as a weekly scheduled job, not only on push, so advisories such as rustls's 2026-09-14 one surface without code changes.
  - `cargo vet` with imported Mozilla/Google/Bytecode Alliance audits and a policy that any **new** crate with a `build.rs` or proc-macro needs a manual look. arrayref attacked through exactly that.
  - Renovate or Dependabot for Cargo and Actions, with a cool-down of a few days before adopting new releases (the arrayref window was under 2 hours).
- **Minimal features.** `image` (jpeg, webp), `reqwest` (rustls, json, gzip only), `tokio` (only the needed features), and no `ratatui-image` protocols that aren't used. Fewer crates means less audit and fewer `build.rs` scripts. Decide between rustls (memory-safe, one stack across OSes; reqwest 0.13's default) and the OS stack. Rustls fits a sibling of tuigram/tuimeta, provided cargo-audit watches it.
- **Actions.** Every `uses:` pinned to a 40-char SHA with a version comment (yt-dlp itself does this). `permissions: {}` at the top level, with `contents: read` per job and `id-token: write`/`attestations: write` only in the release job. No `pull_request_target`. Enable the SHA-pinning policy on the repo. `actions/attest` on release binaries, with the README showing `gh attestation verify`.
- **External binaries are dependencies too.** Track yt-dlp, Deno and mpv versions in a `tools.lock` (version + SHA-256) that tuitube checks at startup, warning or refusing when the binary on disk doesn't match. That turns "whatever is on PATH" into an audited dependency.

### Gaps
- reqwest 0.13's changelog was seen only through secondary copies. Verify on GitHub before relying on its defaults.
- No source assessed whether cargo-vet audit imports would have caught arrayref 0.3.10 in time.

## Sandboxing the subprocesses (yt-dlp + Deno, mpv, tuitube itself) per OS, and network needs

### Takeaway
Practical for one developer in Oct 2026:
- **Linux:** Landlock via the `landlock` crate in a `pre_exec` hook, with filesystem limited to the private dirs and network to TCP 443 by *port*. Hosts need a parent-run allowlisting proxy. Optionally seccomp/bubblewrap.
- **macOS:** `sandbox-exec` with a Seatbelt profile. It's deprecated but still present and working on macOS 27.2 (tested 2026-10-09). It filters network only to `*` or `localhost`, so the same proxy pattern applies.
- **Windows:** an AppContainer with `internetClient` plus an ACL on one folder, and a job object.

Host allowlisting everywhere means routing children through a local CONNECT proxy that allows `www.youtube.com`, `*.googlevideo.com` and `i.ytimg.com`.

### Cited Findings
- **Landlock (kernel docs, 7.3-rc6 build).** ABI 1 dates from Linux 5.13 (filesystem); ABI 2 adds REFER; 3 TRUNCATE; **4 TCP bind/connect by port**; 5 IOCTL_DEV; **6 scoping of abstract UNIX sockets and signals**; 7 logging flags; 8 `RESTRICT_SELF_TSYNC`; **9 `FS_RESOLVE_UNIX` (pathname UNIX sockets)**; **10 UDP bind/connect/send**; 11 `RESTRICT_SELF_NO_NEW_PRIVS`. The object of a network rule is "a TCP or UDP port", with no address or host rules. Files opened before sandboxing are unaffected — [docs.kernel.org Landlock](https://docs.kernel.org/userspace-api/landlock.html). Patch-series numbering differed before merge (an earlier series called pathname-socket scoping "ABI 8"), so trust the merged docs — [LSM archive, Feb 2026](https://kernsec.org/pipermail/linux-security-module-archive/2026-February/055149.html)
- **`sandbox-exec` (Seatbelt) on macOS.** The man page marks it "DEPRECATED", pointing developers to App Sandbox [local test, macOS 27.2, 2026-10-09]. Apple says sandbox-exec "has never been documented for third-party use" (DTS forum) — [Apple Developer Forums 777509](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/777509). It still works under Sequoia — [gastown.dev](https://gastown.dev/docs/research/macos-sandbox-exec). On macOS 26, zsh fails under a deny-default profile because it reads unallowed `hw.*` sysctls, while bash works — [Cursor forum](https://forum.cursor.com/t/sandbox-fails-to-initialize-for-zsh-on-macos-26-tahoe-darwin25-zsh-5-9-reads-unwhitelisted-hw-sysctls/161023)
- **Local tests, macOS 27.2, 2026-10-09:**
  - `/usr/bin/sandbox-exec` is present.
  - `(deny network-outbound (remote ip "*:*"))` makes `curl https://www.youtube.com` fail ("Couldn't connect"), while an allow-all profile returns 200.
  - `(remote ip "www.youtube.com:443")` is rejected with "host must be * or localhost in network address".
  - Allowing only `localhost:*` still blocks direct connections.
  - `(deny file-read* (subpath "$HOME/.ssh"))` makes `ls ~/.ssh` fail with "Operation not permitted".
- **App Sandbox for CLI tools.** An embedded tool can inherit a sandbox with `com.apple.security.app-sandbox` + `com.apple.security.inherit`. Inheritance "doesn't work when a user runs the tool directly from Terminal, because Terminal is not sandboxed", and extra entitlements such as `get-task-allow` make sandbox init trap — [Apple forums 680212](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/680212); [Apple forums 706390](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/706390); [Apple forums 53348](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/53348)
- **Windows AppContainer (Microsoft Learn, updated 2025-09-03).** An app can launch a child in an AppContainer by moniker: `CreateAppContainerProfile` plus `PROC_THREAD_ATTRIBUTE_SECURITY_CAPABILITIES` (and `…ALL_APPLICATION_PACKAGES_POLICY` for LPAC). "Without the *network* capability, an AppContainer cannot access the network". Access is the intersection of user and AppContainer grants, processes run at Low IL, and `LOCALAPPDATA`/`TEMP` are redirected to the profile folder. LPAC needs explicit capabilities even for registry and COM — [Microsoft Learn: Launch an AppContainer](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/secauthz/implementing-an-appcontainer). The `rappct` crate wraps this for Rust — [docs.rs rappct](https://docs.rs/crate/rappct/latest)
- **Hosts the YouTube extractor uses.** yt-dlp hardcodes Innertube on `www.youtube.com` (`music.youtube.com` for the music client) and fetches thumbnails from `i.ytimg.com`. Media URLs (googlevideo) come from responses — [yt-dlp youtube/_base.py](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/yt_dlp/extractor/youtube/_base.py). The Deno runtime needs no network when `--remote-components` is off (`--no-remote`, `--cached-only`, no `--allow-net`) — [deno.py](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/yt_dlp/extractor/youtube/jsc/_builtin/deno.py)

### Inferences
- **What each process needs (verify empirically with a logging proxy):**

  | Process | Network | Files read | Files written | Exec |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | yt-dlp (Python) | `www.youtube.com`, `music.youtube.com` (if used), `*.googlevideo.com` (only if it downloads), `i.ytimg.com` (only if thumbnails are requested; better for tuitube to fetch thumbnails itself) | its install, Python stdlib, CA certs, the 0600 cookie file if logged in | private cache dir, cookie file (rotation) | Deno only |
  | Deno (child of yt-dlp) | none | its binary, DENO_DIR cache | V8 code cache disabled (`--no-code-cache`); none expected | none |
  | mpv | `*.googlevideo.com` (incl. `manifest.googlevideo.com`) | its install, fonts, GPU/audio device nodes | none, given `--no-config` and no watch-later | none |
  | tuitube | the proxy itself plus `i.ytimg.com`/`yt3.ggpht.com` for thumbnails | its data dir, terminal | its 0700 data dir | yt-dlp, mpv, `open` |

- **The proxy pattern (the same as the tuigram report's recommendation for the Meta helper).** tuitube runs a CONNECT proxy on a Unix socket or loopback port that allows hosts by suffix. Children get `HTTPS_PROXY=http://127.0.0.1:<port>`, and their direct outbound connections are denied: by Landlock TCP-connect rules allowing only the proxy port on Linux, by `(allow network-outbound (remote ip "localhost:<port>"))` on macOS, and on Windows by AppContainer with `internetClient`, which can't filter hosts, so the proxy is advisory there unless a firewall rule is added. yt-dlp honours `--proxy` and passes proxies to Deno; mpv honours `--http-proxy` for http, but check its HTTPS/CONNECT behaviour. The proxy also gives one place to log every host contacted, which is useful for the privacy audit.
- **Linux.** In `Command::pre_exec`: `prctl(PR_SET_NO_NEW_PRIVS)`, then a Landlock ruleset (best effort with `landlock` crate compat levels). Read-only: binary dirs, `/usr`, `/etc/ssl`, `/etc/resolv.conf` (unless DNS also goes through the proxy). Read-write: the private dir. Net: connect only to the proxy port. Scope: abstract unix and signals (ABI 6+). Then a seccomp filter denying `ptrace`, `process_vm_*` and (for mpv) `execve` after start. bubblewrap (`bwrap --unshare-all --share-net …`) is an alternative where installed, but Landlock needs no setuid helper. Never sandbox the main TUI with `execve` denied: it must launch mpv and `open`.
- **macOS.** Generate a Seatbelt profile per child: `(deny default)` is fragile across OS updates (the zsh sysctl breakage), so start from `(allow default)` and deny `file-read*`/`file-write*` on `$HOME` except the private dirs and the needed install paths, deny `network-outbound` except `localhost:<proxyport>`, and deny `process-exec` except the Deno path for yt-dlp. Launch it as `sandbox-exec -f profile.sb -D …`, still with no shell. Wrapping a Homebrew mpv via `sandbox-exec` is the only option, since it can't be re-signed with App Sandbox entitlements, and inheritance doesn't apply to a TUI started from Terminal. Plan for removal: if `sandbox-exec` disappears, fall back loudly (a status-bar warning), never silently.
- **Windows.** Launch yt-dlp and mpv in an AppContainer with `internetClient` only, grant the profile folder (or one ACL'd data folder) read/write, and put each child in a job object with `JOB_OBJECT_LIMIT_KILL_ON_JOB_CLOSE` so nothing outlives tuitube. mpv may need GPU and audio access, which AppContainers sometimes block; test, and fall back to a restricted token plus a job object.
- **Order of work for one developer.** (1) Hardened argv, env, CWD and config flags: cheap and covers most real risk. (2) Allowlisting proxy plus logging. (3) Landlock on Linux. (4) Seatbelt on macOS. (5) AppContainer on Windows. Treat sandboxing as defence in depth, not a reason to skip (1).

### Gaps
- Not verified: mpv's behaviour inside an AppContainer (GPU/WASAPI access) and under `HTTPS_PROXY`/`--http-proxy` for TLS googlevideo URLs.
- No Landlock ABI-to-kernel-release table was confirmed beyond 5.13 (ABI 1). Check `landlock_create_ruleset(NULL,0,LANDLOCK_CREATE_RULESET_VERSION)` at runtime.
- Apple has announced no removal date for `sandbox-exec`, and its future is unknown.

## Privacy signals: what YouTube learns from an unofficial client, and where the single gate goes

### Takeaway
YouTube's "read receipt" equivalent is the **playback-tracking pings** (`videostatsPlaybackUrl` / `videostatsWatchtimeUrl` from the player response's `playbackTracking`). They feed watch history and view counts. yt-dlp sends them only with `--mark-watched` (off by default). mpv's ytdl_hook doesn't request them. YouTube.js sends them only when the app calls `addToWatchHistory()`/`updateWatchTime()`. rustypipe has no code that sends them at all. YouTube still sees every Innertube request and googlevideo stream fetch from the user's IP, and guest sessions are tied together by visitor data / `__Secure-YNID` and PO-token bindings. Design: one gated function that may ever send a history ping, off by default and only for an explicit "logged-in + sync history" setting; ephemeral or rotated visitor data; local history in a 0600 file that can be switched off; an optional proxy or Invidious, with the trust shift stated plainly.

### Cited Findings
- **yt-dlp `_mark_watched`.** It generates a random 16-char `cpn` and requests `playbackTracking.videostatsPlaybackUrl` and `videostatsWatchtimeUrl`, with the comment "these seem to mark watchtime 'history' in the real world". `--mark-watched` "Mark videos watched (even with `--simulate`)"; `--no-mark-watched` is the default — [yt-dlp youtube/_video.py](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/yt_dlp/extractor/youtube/_video.py); [yt-dlp README](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/README.md)
- **YouTube.js.** `MediaInfo.addToWatchHistory()` and `updateWatchTime(startTime)` take `playback_tracking.videostats_playback_url` / `videostats_watchtime_url` (rewriting `https://s.` to `https://www.`) and are explicit methods, not automatic — [YouTube.js src/core/mixins/MediaInfo.ts](https://github.com/LuanRT/YouTube.js/blob/main/src/core/mixins/MediaInfo.ts)
- **rustypipe.** A grep of the 2026-08-03 source for `videostats`, `watchtime`, `ptracking` and `playbackTracking` finds nothing. It can *read* playback history and subscriptions with the `userdata` feature when authenticated — [rustypipe source](https://codeberg.org/ThetaDev/rustypipe); [docs.rs rustypipe](https://docs.rs/rustypipe)
- **mpv.** ytdl_hook's yt-dlp command has no `--mark-watched` (raw options could add one) — [ytdl_hook.lua](https://github.com/mpv-player/mpv/blob/master/player/lua/ytdl_hook.lua). mpv itself writes watch-later state locally under `~/.local/state/mpv/watch_later/` — [mpv.rst](https://github.com/mpv-player/mpv/blob/master/DOCS/man/mpv.rst)
- **Visitor tracking.** VISITOR_INFO1_LIVE was replaced by `__Secure-YNID` (6 months), and YSC is per session — [Google cookies page](https://policies.google.com/technologies/cookies). PO tokens are bound to "the user session (Visitor ID or account Session ID) or to the video ID" — [yt-dlp PO Token Guide](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/wiki/PO-Token-Guide). yt-dlp's web PO-token cache key includes the client IP (`innertube_context.client.remoteHost`) — [webpo_cachespec.py](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/yt_dlp/extractor/youtube/pot/_builtin/webpo_cachespec.py)
- **Rate limits link a session's activity.** Guest sessions hit about 300 videos/hour (about 1000 page/player requests); accounts about 2000 videos/hour. The wiki suggests 5–10 s sleeps — [yt-dlp wiki: Extractors](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/wiki/Extractors)
- **IP exposure.** Invidious: "By default, the video stream is fetched directly from Google's servers", and then "Google will be able to see your IP address". With "Proxy videos" on (off by default) the instance relays traffic, but "instance owners can log your IP address", and account data (watch history, subscriptions) lives in the instance's database — [Invidious FAQ](https://docs.invidious.io/faq/). Invidious's Companion (stable integration in v2.20250913.0, built on YouTube.js) handles PO tokens. Self-hosters report breakage and IP rotation needs in 2025 — [Invidious v2.20250913.0](https://git.hackliberty.org/iv-org/invidious/src/v2.20250913.0); [TrueNAS forum, Nov 2025](https://forums.truenas.com/t/invidious-unable-to-generate-a-potoken/58073)
- **The accountless norm.** FreeTube: "While YouTube can still see your video requests, it can no longer track you using cookies or JavaScript. Your subscriptions, playlists and history are stored locally on your computer and never sent out" — [FreeTube README](https://github.com/FreeTubeApp/FreeTube/blob/development/README.md). NewPipe: "you don't need an account on any service to use NewPipe", and subscriptions work "without logging into any account" — [NewPipe README](https://github.com/TeamNewPipe/NewPipe/blob/dev/README.md)

### Inferences
- **Signal table for tuitube (mirroring tuigram's):**

  | Signal | What YouTube learns | Rule |
  |---|---|---|
  | Watch-history / watchtime ping | Video watched, how long, tied to the account or visitor | Only from `App::report_watch` (sending `videostats*` via yt-dlp `--mark-watched` or a direct request), only when logged in **and** "sync watch history" is on (default off), only after real playback (mpv reports `playback-time` ≥ N s with the window focused), never during prefetch, search previews or autoplay |
  | Innertube `player`/`next` requests | Which video was *opened*, from which IP and visitor ID | Unavoidable for playback. Never prefetch player responses for items merely highlighted; resolve only on Enter |
  | Search | Query text | Accountless by default. If logged in, warn that searches can land in the account's history; consider always searching without cookies |
  | Browse feeds (subscriptions) | Channel list, if fetched with an account | Prefer local subscriptions fetched per channel accountless (RSS/Innertube). This reveals the list as a pattern from one IP, but not tied to an account |
  | Thumbnails | Which items were *displayed* (`i.ytimg.com` hits from the user's IP) | Optional, lazy (visible rows only), never for hidden or preview items; a "no thumbnails" privacy mode |
  | Likes, subscribe, comments | Explicit actions | Not in v1 (they need an account); if ever added, only from an explicit keypress plus confirm |

- **Visitor data.** Without cookies, yt-dlp still gets visitor data per run. Rotate it, don't persist it: run yt-dlp with `--no-cache-dir` or a cache cleared per session, and give tuitube's own Innertube client, if any, a fresh visitor ID per launch. Trade-off: persistent visitor data reduces bot checks, while fresh data reduces linkability. Make it a setting with "fresh per launch" as the default.
- **Proxying and Invidious.** These move trust from Google to an instance operator, who sees the same things plus the IP, and public instances are unreliable in 2025–2026. Offer a user-configured HTTP(S)/SOCKS proxy (e.g. their own VPN) for all children, which the sandbox proxy design already supports, and treat Invidious as an optional backend, not a privacy guarantee.
- **Local history.** Store it in the data dir (0600 via rename, like `Settings::save`), with a setting to disable it, a "pause history" key and "clear history". Keep mpv's watch-later off (`--no-config` already prevents `save-position-on-quit`). If resume is wanted, store positions in tuitube's own 0600 file keyed by video ID.
- **Tests.** As in tuigram, a fake yt-dlp/mpv in tests can assert that `--mark-watched` and `videostats` URLs never appear in argv or proxy logs unless the gate fired.

### Gaps
- Whether a signed-in Innertube `player` request alone (without the `videostats` pings) adds to watch history wasn't found in any source. yt-dlp's comment suggests the pings are what matter.
- Whether YouTube records search history for signed-in Innertube searches from non-browser clients is unverified.
- The meaning of googlevideo's `ip=` URL parameter (streams bound to the requesting IP) is commonly stated but no authoritative source was found. Only yt-dlp's PO-token cache key including the client IP was confirmed.
- TLS and HTTP fingerprinting of yt-dlp versus browsers: not researched in depth.

## Account lock or ban risk for logged-in unofficial use

### Takeaway
The risk is real, unquantified, and falls on the **Google account**: YouTube's terms allow suspending "your Google account". yt-dlp's own wiki warns of temporary or permanent bans and recommends a throwaway account. 2024–2026 reports are anecdotal and involve heavy use (hundreds of downloads a day, cookie rotation across many accounts). Most "Sign in to confirm you're not a bot" errors are IP or rate-limit blocks, not account bans. No source measures the risk for light interactive use. Accountless by default, with a throwaway-account warning for any opt-in login, is the defensible stance.

### Cited Findings
- **yt-dlp wiki.** Logging in means "you run the risk of it being banned (temporarily or permanently)… Use it only when necessary, or consider using a throwaway account" — [yt-dlp wiki: Extractors](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/wiki/Extractors)
- **YouTube ToS (effective 2023-12-15).** These terms prohibit "automated means" and circumvention, and YouTube "reserves the right to suspend or terminate your Google account" — [YouTube Terms](https://www.youtube.com/t/terms)
- **Issue #16147 (2026-03-06).** A user downloading "hundreds of audio files per day" was rate-limited after about 600 downloads, switched to cookies from a "newly registered Google account", and it was "quickly banned by Google". No maintainer reply — [yt-dlp #16147](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/issues/16147)
- **Issue #11447.** A user with 50–100 cookie sets reported YouTube blocking accounts after about 100 requests, and said "the accounts from the cookies are of course also blocked… even… premium accounts" — [yt-dlp #11447](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/issues/11447)
- **Issue #15724 (2026-01-28).** It asks whether a YouTube ban affects Gmail, Drive or Docs and whether anyone was permanently banned, and has no visible answer — [yt-dlp #15724](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/issues/15724)
- **Community advice.** A guide warns of "a small chance your account could be banned" and advises "not to login with an important Google account (such as an account you use for Gmail)" — [gamer191 gist](https://gist.github.com/gamer191/ddf0b23b0a6df8e2ffe81bd1dda9154c). Pinchflat's wiki (March 2025) says yt-dlp "no longer recommends using YouTube cookies since they can cause YouTube to issue IP bans" — [Pinchflat wiki](https://github.com/kieraneglin/pinchflat/wiki/YouTube-Cookies). A Linux Mint forum post (June 2024) relays unverified reports of bans after cookie workarounds — [Linux Mint Forums](https://forums.linuxmint.com/viewtopic.php?t=421647)
- **IP blocks vs. account bans.** Users attribute bot-check errors to IP blocks, and some resolved them by waiting a few days — [yt-dlp #12264](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/issues/12264); [yt-dlp #12045](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/issues/12045). Missing PO tokens may lead to "your account or IP address being blocked" — [PO Token Guide](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/wiki/PO-Token-Guide)
- **Precedent from mainstream unofficial clients.** FreeTube and NewPipe avoid accounts altogether ([FreeTube README](https://github.com/FreeTubeApp/FreeTube/blob/development/README.md); [NewPipe README](https://github.com/TeamNewPipe/NewPipe/blob/dev/README.md)). SmartTube, which does offer Google sign-in, made news in 2025 for a supply-chain compromise, not for bans — [CyberInsider](https://cyberinsider.com/popular-youtube-app-for-android-tv-smarttube-compromised-with-malware/)

### Inferences
- **Behaviour that lowers risk (inferred from the rate-limit guidance and ban anecdotes).** Interactive pacing only: no bulk resolution, no prefetching player responses, at most one extraction per user action. Never rotate across multiple accounts. Never retry hard on 403/429/bot-check: back off and tell the user instead (the same "don't hammer a rejected connection" rule as the tuigram report). Keep `--mark-watched` off.
- **UI copy for an opt-in login** (parallel to tuimeta's `ui::NOT_META`): "tuitube isn't YouTube's. Signing in uses your Google session; YouTube's terms allow it to suspend your *Google account*. Use a separate account, not the one with your email."
- **Use the official API for account data.** If account features are wanted (subscriptions import, playlists), the official Data API with `youtube.readonly` and the user's own Cloud project is the ToS-compliant path and carries far less ban risk than replaying browser cookies. It just can't play videos.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies account-ban rates for low-volume, interactive, cookie-authenticated use. No 2024–2026 report was found of a main (Gmail) account losing access purely from light yt-dlp, mpv or SmartTube use.
- Whether a YouTube-side action ever extends to the whole Google account in practice (beyond what the ToS allows) remains undocumented.
